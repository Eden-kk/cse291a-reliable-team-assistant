Reliable Team Assistant: Learning, Recovery and Scheduling under API Budgets
Option 5: Failure Propagation and Recovery in Multi-Agent Systems

Motivation. A team assistant investigating concurrent incidents must incorporate new operational knowledge without spreading mistakes or blocking urgent requests. I will study how versioned procedural memory, dependency-aware recovery and budget-aware scheduling jointly maintain useful, evidence-grounded responses. Alert investigation is the evaluation workload; resource inspection and Q&A share the intended product architecture.

Integrated system. Configuration/status and telemetry/dependency specialists query replayable tools; a coordinator consumes their outputs and original observations. A shared execution layer records actual inputs, memory/source versions, state reads and published responses. Invalid knowledge can contaminate multiple incidents; recovery invalidates affected work, preserves verified independent work and rejects obsolete writes. Published messages remain in an append-only ledger with linked corrections.

Self-evolution. A learner proposes scoped diagnostic procedures from resolved development episodes and verified evidence, such as checking configuration events when a symptom recurs. A clue is not automatically a causal rule. Rules guide tool selection and diagnosis; candidates undergo independent development regression checks before versioned promotion or rollback. Episodes used to construct or select memory remain outside final evaluation. Ordinary improvements apply to future investigations; identified erroneous versions trigger targeted invalidation of active dependent work. This studies procedural-memory learning, not weight training or unrestricted self-modification.

Multi-group scheduling. New investigations, recovery and memory-validation jobs share bounded request/token capacity. A scheduler reserves capacity for initial responses, applies priority with aging and per-group fairness, and queues background learning. It schedules at call boundaries; in-flight requests consume resources even if abandoned. The system records useful first-response and recovery deadlines, starvation and budget exhaustion; overload admission and completion priorities are tested.

Hypotheses. H1: Selective replay preserves quality and reduces work when reusable work exceeds validation/tracking cost. H2: Actual-input and memory-version tracking limits cross-incident contamination better than citation-only tracking. H3: Capacity reservation and fairness protect urgent response latency under recovery bursts, with explicit costs to background work. H4: Gated memory improves subsequent evidence-complete investigations or tool efficiency relative to frozen memory, while limiting harmful updates.

Evaluation. Cloud-OpsBench supplies existing snapshots, tools and native scoring; labels remain outside inference. Construct only workflow faults, arrival schedules and simulated publication state. Compare restart/selective recovery under matched consumed-fault prefixes; always-verify checks intermediates before consumption and is evaluated end-to-end. Use staged ablations: frozen/gated/ungated memory; FIFO/fair scheduling; restart/selective recovery. Hold other components fixed, then test their interaction under an erroneous shared-memory update and concurrent arrivals. Include dense contamination, incomplete provenance and failed runs. Report diagnosis/evidence quality, contamination, unsafe actions, latency, calls/tokens and tracking costs. New frozen cases and measured provider usage determine sample size.

Feasibility and delivery. A learned policy passed three regression cases and matched frozen memory on two of three fresh cases, without observed improvement; a harmful control was rejected. Thirty-six known-cost trace replays exposed response/recovery tradeoffs and overload failures. Six state tests covered scoped revocation and rollback. These are component pilots, not integrated live-API validation. Phase 1 (October 28) delivers concurrent workflows, memory lifecycle, faults and baselines; Phase 2 (December 10) delivers selective recovery and component/interaction evaluation. Local replay needs no GPU; course API access and costs require verification.
