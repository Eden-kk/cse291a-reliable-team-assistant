Reliable Team Assistant: Safe Recovery under Shared-State Changes
CSE291A Systems for LLMs and AI Agents — Option 5; solo

Question and workload. When evidence or a diagnostic rule becomes invalid after an oncall team assistant has consumed it, can dependency-aware recovery preserve answer quality while reducing recomputation? Configuration and telemetry specialists query existing Cloud-OpsBench snapshots; a coordinator consumes their observations. A configuration update may invalidate one branch while leaving another reusable. The project characterizes this boundary rather than assuming that more agents or selective recovery always help.

Explicit boundary. Core experiments begin with a trusted invalidation event identifying a source/version or artifact, not the correct diagnosis. Automatic discovery of arbitrary errors is outside scope. A human clue such as “configuration just changed” requests fresh evidence; it is not itself proof of a causal diagnosis. Snapshot transitions and workflow faults are constructed interventions, not recorded production histories. Tools are read-only; publication and correction are local simulated records. Crash recovery and real operational permissions are outside the evaluated claims.

Required mechanism. Track actual consumed inputs and their versions, invalidate the affected dependency closure, preserve valid independent work, reject obsolete commits, and retain linked corrections. Observable incomplete provenance triggers conservative restart. Controlled tests verify recomputation, cumulative budgets and obsolete-write rejection; semantic recovery availability still requires real-model evaluation. Unrecorded implicit dependencies are not automatically detectable. Shared memory is a versioned input in the core; learning that memory is a separate extension.

Required evaluation. Compare no recovery, full restart, and selective recovery after the same consumed-fault prefix, with the same notification and logical resource ceilings. Compare consumption-time source-consistency verification separately from the beginning of the workflow, because it may prevent the faulty prefix. This verifier checks accessible source records, not arbitrary semantic truth. Measure all scheduled conditions: root correctness, native evidence completeness, source fidelity, contract validity, stale-publication events, failures, calls, tokens, tools, and end-to-end latency. Then report recovery increments and quality-preserving paired savings as secondary analyses. Include tracking and verification overhead; recorded subscription tokens are not API-dollar costs.

Core studies. (1) Local stale evidence and incorrect intermediate content, recording whether each injection was applied, consumed, propagated, and corrected. (2) Sparse versus dense dependencies and missing provenance, including cases where selective recovery reduces to restart. (3) Parallel versus telemetry-first collection: useful evidence handoff may improve initial diagnosis while increasing the recovery closure. Development tests precede a bounded newly frozen evaluation; repeats do not count as new incidents. Labels remain outside inference. Failures and non-propagating injections are retained.

Priority and milestones. Must-have: finish propagation/recovery baselines and a reproducible Phase 1 package; complete development controls and overhead measurements by November 12; freeze the final protocol and run held-out recovery evaluation by November 23; finish analysis by November 30 and final artifact by December 10. These are planning targets, with course submission dates checked against current instructions. First extension: compare FIFO and one bounded admission/reservation policy under concurrent new investigations and recovery, reporting completed quality-qualified answers, correction latency, queue wait, rejection and budget failures. Second extension: independently gated procedural-memory proposals and revocation, including generation/validation cost and rejected candidates. Neither extension blocks the core deliverable. One combined demonstration is optional.

Feasibility and exit conditions. Existing development runs show factual contamination and correction and smaller recovery work in selected independent-branch cases; they do not establish general superiority. One E5 pair recovered successfully under both methods while both new foreground investigations were uncertain. The memory candidate failed its complete-batch gate and remains inactive. Proceed only after independent expected invalidation sets, matched accounting, and post-inference quality scoring work. Reduce predeclared matrix size before execution if resources are insufficient; do not discard failures or select held-out cases by outcomes. Deliver a reproducible explanation of when reuse is safe, when its overhead dominates, and when restart or source verification is preferable.

Latest development evidence (October24). Two already-seen runtime/performance incidents completed26 real calls with no retries. Selective recovery, full restart and provenance fallback pass native and current-evidence gates on both; selective reduces incremental task tokens62.06%/55.33% versus restart. Independent source-consistency prevention also passes and costs less end-to-end. Both cases concern the same application and sparse configuration dependencies; these are feasibility observations, not general superiority. Dense-consumption and wrong-content semantic controls remain the next required studies; no held-out cases have been opened.
